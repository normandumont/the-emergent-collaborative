## 1. Recommended direction

Develop **Concept A: Fieldnotes Laboratory** for the first high-fidelity exploration: a calm editorial page with warm neutral surfaces, strong reading typography, disciplined annotations, and a small amount of abstract trajectory-based illustration.

It should feel like an open working notebook from a thoughtful research-and-technology initiative: human enough to welcome questions, precise enough to expose limitations, and exploratory without pretending discoveries have already been made.

The visual balance is:

- **Research laboratory:** traceability, legible references, careful distinctions, and visible uncertainty.
- **Technology incubator:** practical curiosity, clear work-in-progress descriptions, and a restrained technical vocabulary.
- **Human exploration project:** approachable reading, a concise origin, and invitations to contribute without disclosing health information.

These are design intentions, not claims of institutional status. The site must not impersonate a laboratory, university, clinic, established team, or operational software platform.

## 3. Visual design principles

### 3.1 Explanation before atmosphere

Visitors should understand the purpose before noticing an illustration technique. The roughly 20-second comprehension goal depends on legible copy and proximity of key facts, not on a cinematic opening.

The name, early-stage label, working headline, definition, "Parkinson's: our first initiative," and non-clinical statement must read as one introductory package. Never hide the boundary in a hover state or push it below a large visual.

### 3.2 Precision without institutional theater

Use alignment, readable metadata, thoughtful rules, and source context to suggest care. Do not manufacture credibility through seals, medical crosses, fake paper abstracts, accreditation-like badges, staff portraits, or laboratory stock photographs.

### 3.3 Human, not sentimental

Warmth should come from language, comfortable reading, and respectful participation. Avoid images of distress, heroic patient narratives, inspirational treatment promises, or a founder-as-patient conversion story.

### 3.4 Technical, not performative

Instrumentation should help orientation: a modest section index or a clear verification date is useful. Fictional telemetry, random coordinates, hexadecimal decoration, sensor traces, and meaningless node networks are not.

### 3.5 Distinct concepts, honest relationships

Evidence, Experience, and Exploration deserve equally legible treatment, but they are not equivalent forms of proof. Their wording must state the differences; visual symmetry cannot imply equal scientific authority.

### 3.6 Maturity stays visible

An attractive presentation must not make an internal content draft look like shipped software. Current status, scope, limitations, and date travel together.

### 3.7 Restraint creates room for inquiry

Prefer a small number of consistent surfaces, accents, type roles, and graphic gestures. Do not make every section a card, every sentence a callout, or every transition a new visual effect.

## 4. Layout philosophy

### 4.1 A reading spine, not a control surface

Use one primary text spine with stable left alignment and an optional subordinate annotation/illustration margin on larger screens. Do not alternate text left/right between sections or make a reader zigzag through the narrative.

P1-P9 keep their approved sequence. Wider compositions may add breathing room, not a second independent stream of essential content. A date, status, or limitation that changes interpretation must remain adjacent to its item rather than being banished to a distant side rail.

The prototype's editorial review notes are not website content. Do not reproduce "Purpose," "Classification," and other internal annotations as a public dashboard.

### 4.2 Starting dimensions for mockup exploration

These are proposed design ranges, not implementation specifications or verified accessibility outcomes.

| Element | Starting direction |
|---|---|
| Overall desktop content envelope | Approximately 1,080-1,200 logical/CSS-equivalent pixels, centered with generous outer space. |
| Main paragraph measure | Roughly 60-70 characters per line, shortened rather than stretched across wide monitors. |
| Mobile outer inset | Approximately 20-24 logical pixels; may reduce to 16 at very narrow widths to preserve readable text. |
| Desktop section separation | Roughly 64-88 pixels, with smaller internal spacing that shows related content belongs together. |
| Mobile section separation | Roughly 40-56 pixels; avoid a sequence of oversized empty screens. |
| Related label-to-copy spacing | Roughly 8-16 pixels; do not visually detach a status or warning from the content it qualifies. |
| Primary control height | Aim for at least 44 logical/CSS-equivalent pixels with comfortable separation, subject to actual target geometry. |

Keep the introductory block content-sized, not viewport-height. Do not force all five facts onto one screen by shrinking text, or force a "hero moment" that hides them below decoration.

### 4.3 Section rhythm

Alternate emphasis through hierarchy rather than constant background changes:

- P1: strongest type hierarchy, with restrained decorative support.
- P2-P3: comfortable explanation and the three named concepts.
- P4: a clearly framed first-initiative statement and question.
- P5: quiet prose, not a promotional founder feature.
- P6: factual work note with status and limitations.
- P7: a gently differentiated participation area, not a sales banner.
- P8-P9: compact but fully legible source/accountability material.

Avoid full-bleed dark sections merely to create contrast. A subtle surface shift at P4 or P7 can help orientation; a change at every section creates fragmentation.

## 5. Typography direction

### 5.1 Recommended typographic character

For Fieldnotes Laboratory, explore a **readable contemporary serif for major headings** and a **humanist sans serif for body, navigation, actions, and source metadata**.

The serif should be sturdy and open, not high-contrast, hairline, ornamental, or reminiscent of a medical journal masthead. The sans should have clear letterforms, distinguishable characters, and enough warmth to avoid an enterprise-software feel.

The organization name uses ordinary text typography. Do not customize its lettering into a wordmark or add a standalone emblem.

Alternative concepts use different type roles, described in section 15. Font families are deliberately not finalized. Any later shortlist must consider readability, available weights, licensing, permitted local delivery, and fallback behavior. Do not depend on an unreviewed remote font service.

### 5.2 Scale and text behavior

| Role | Proposed mockup direction |
|---|---|
| Main headline | Approximately 42-56 pixels desktop and 30-36 mobile; medium weight and natural wrapping. Reduce oversized display styling before compressing the definition. |
| Major section headings | Approximately 28-34 desktop and 24-28 mobile, clearly below the main headline. |
| Concept/item subheadings | Approximately 21-24; consistent across Evidence / Experience / Exploration. |
| Body | Start at approximately 18 pixels with 1.5-1.65 line height; user resizing must remain possible. |
| Metadata, source context, notices | Approximately 16-17 pixels with comfortable line height; do not reduce essential warnings to caption size. |
| Navigation and actions | Clearly readable sans serif, with visible labels and generous interactive area. |

Use sentence case. Avoid long all-capital labels, wide letter spacing, condensed fonts, and paragraphs in monospace. A narrow use of a technical type style for a nonessential section index is possible, but dates, status descriptions, email addresses, and sources must not require reading terminal-like text.

Do not hard-code line breaks around the working headline. Test its current length and a plausible longer revision. Body copy must remain stable when fonts fall back, text spacing changes, or text is enlarged.

## 6. Spacing and density

Use a simple spacing rhythm based on a small unit, approximately 4 pixels, with common intervals such as 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, and 64. These are design aids, not a required token implementation.

Reading density should be **moderate and editorial**:

- Enough space to separate concepts, not so much that context is lost between screens.
- Short paragraphs and restrained lists, following the content prototype.
- Clear grouping of a heading, its explanation, and its limitation.
- More breathing room around the first-initiative question and collaboration warning.
- No dense multi-column source tables, tiny status strips, or overpacked legal footer.

Do not solve long-page fatigue by hiding essential copy in accordions. First simplify presentation, reduce decoration, and use the approved section map.

## 7. Color-system strategy

### 7.1 Functional roles before palette identity

Define a small semantic set: page surface, secondary surface, primary text, secondary text, functional boundary, interactive accent, hover/visited treatment, focus indicator, and contextual caution.

The first direction uses warm off-white, charcoal, and deep ink/teal with a restrained earth accent. It should not become a medical-blue site with a warm background added.

Illustrative starting colors for Concept A:

| Role | Candidate value | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| Page surface | Warm paper `#F7F5EF` | Main reading surface. |
| Secondary surface | Pale mineral `#ECEFEA` | Occasional section grouping, not every paragraph. |
| Primary text | Charcoal `#222A2B` | Headings, body, important boundaries. |
| Secondary text | Slate `#4C5B5C` | Dates and context that remain readable. |
| Interactive accent | Deep teal `#185D62` | Underlined links and a limited number of action surfaces. |
| Reflective accent | Earth/clay `#854B37` | Small illustration detail or subordinate emphasis, not a medical/status code. |

These are exploration candidates, **not a contrast-certified palette**. Focus, hover, active, visited, and control states must be designed and checked on their actual adjacent surfaces before approval. Do not infer accessibility from a hex value or from a normal-state pair.

### 7.2 Rules

Keep most of the page neutral. Use accent to indicate an action or one deliberate emphasis, not to flood every section.

Do not assign green to Evidence, amber to Experience, and red to Exploration. That would introduce unsupported quality or safety rankings. Project-status labels also avoid a red/amber/green maturity scale.

Links need underlines or another clear non-color cue. Focus needs a conspicuous outline or equivalent boundary that works on light surfaces and dark controls, not merely a slight hue change.

Functional outlines and essential graphic elements must meet applicable contrast requirements. Purely decorative rules may be softer only when they communicate no necessary boundary or meaning.

Start with one light appearance. A dark-mode variant or theme toggle is not part of the initial concept; reconsider only with a demonstrated need and a complete accessible palette, not as an automatic extra feature. User high-contrast/forced-colors settings must remain usable.

## 8. Imagery and illustration strategy

### 8.1 First-concept baseline: no photography dependency

Use typography, spacing, and one restrained abstract illustration to establish character. The design should still feel complete if that illustration is removed.

Candidate illustration: a few separate, non-data trajectories or contour-like paths, placed beside the desktop introduction or in a quiet margin. Use an irregular but deliberate composition, without axes, values, anatomy, sensor motifs, endpoints labeled as discoveries, or a convergent "answer" node.

This is an editorial gesture about inquiry, not measured change. It must not resemble an ECG, tremor trace, gait assessment, symptom curve, or scientific result. If the image cannot be reliably read as decoration, remove or explicitly contextualize it rather than borrowing scientific authority.

Do not connect the three concepts into a pipeline ending in a medical conclusion. Do not illustrate public research and private personal records as a merged cloud, shared database, or unguarded stream.

### 8.2 Visual reference families

| Reference | Appropriate interpretation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Trajectories and motion | Static directional paths suggesting a question unfolding. | Not a real journey route, disease progression curve, or treatment-response line. |
| Maps | Abstract contours, spacing, and orientation cues. | No identifiable location traces, invented geographic coordinates, or tracking interface. |
| Scientific diagrams | Clear labels, consistent alignment, disciplined relationships. | Do not fabricate data or reproduce a scientific figure without permission, attribution, and context. |
| Signals and emerging patterns | Sparse repetitions or a small controlled variation in a decorative line field. | No live waveform, heartbeat reference, or implication that a system has detected a pattern. |
| Restrained instrumentation | Meaningful section index, date, scope, or source notation. | No arbitrary readings, scan effects, console chrome, or "system online" language. |

### 8.3 Photography, if later justified

An authentic, permissioned image of making or exploration could support the origin, but it is not required for the first high-fidelity concept. No stock doctors, clinic interiors, laboratory coats, staged brain imagery, or founder portrait dominating P1.

A ride photograph would need review for bystanders, location disclosure, context, and publication permission. Do not use raw routes or health screenshots. Its caption must identify personal context, not imply evidence of benefit.

Decorative imagery should be omitted from the assistive-technology reading sequence; meaningful imagery needs useful text alternatives and appropriate captions. A picture must not carry essential boundaries unavailable in text.

## 9. Evidence / Experience / Exploration treatment

Present the three concepts as **three consecutive editorial modules**, with the same heading level, comparable typography, and a consistent question/explanation/limitation structure.

In the recommended concept:

- Use the full words, not initials or icon-only representations.
- A small consistent rule or section marker provides recognition.
- Keep all three modules in one reading column on desktop and mobile.
- Use the written distinctions, not badge colors, to communicate scientific roles.
- Maintain equivalent reading comfort without implying equivalent evidentiary authority.

Do not draw arrows from Experience to Evidence, suggest that an AI process upgrades anecdotes into proof, or style Evidence as an approval stamp.

Classification is not project status. "Experience" explains a source/perspective; "Prototype" describes an artifact's maturity. They must not share an ambiguous icon system.

No three-product card deck, tab switcher, quiz, or hover interaction is needed. If later comparison suggests a horizontal treatment, it must preserve complete copy, order, and context without truncation; the approved first concept remains a vertical reading sequence.

## 10. Project-status labels and current work

P6 should look like an honest work note, not a portfolio of shipped applications.

Use a compact neutral label such as **Prototype — internal written draft** next to the item name, followed immediately by scope, limitation, and verification date. The label must have sufficient contrast, wrap naturally, and remain readable without a tooltip.

Use a separate subordinate heading for **Possible later directions**, with **Idea** stated in text. Future possibilities must not gain large product art, simulated screens, feature checklists, disabled launch buttons, or readiness percentages.

| Status | Visual treatment | Do not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | Neutral text label, with explicitly conditional copy. | Committed roadmap item or promised delivery. |
| Prototype | Neutral outlined or lightly surfaced label with scope in ordinary text. | Working public service, clinically validated tool, or available app. |
| Active development | Same family of label with a date and defined scope, only when substantiated. | Animated activity, completion percentage, or current software work without confirmation. |
| Experiment | Same family; question and limitation nearby. | Successful result, patient trial, or ethics approval. |
| Operational | Same family; precise supported use and ownership. | Medical validation, regulated clearance, or universal availability. |

Only Idea and the internal content Prototype are relevant to the first concept. Do not show all statuses as a staircase or progress legend that suggests planned advancement.

No green availability dot, live pulse, certification shield, or "verified" seal. A documentation verification date is a factual note, not a quality endorsement.

Internal placeholders must look visibly unresolved in a mockup review layer. Do not style a fake artifact link or email as a working service to make a composition look finished.

## 11. Source and citation links

Retain P8, but for the first concept it can remain a short explanation of source standards if no substantive scientific findings are included. The optional Parkinson's variability sentence and its SOURCE-PD-01 entry are omitted.

Do not add fabricated references simply to make the site look scientific. Existing project documents establish organizational decisions, not medical findings.

For any later supported claim:

- Place a descriptively labeled source link near the claim, not only a tiny superscript number.
- Use normal readable type, underlining, and visible focus.
- Show source identity, author/organization, year, type, and relevant limitations in P8.
- Distinguish an original-source link from a return-to-claim action.
- Keep source context and limitations readable, not in low-contrast footnotes.
- Avoid citation-count badges, evidence scores, star ratings, authority shields, or previews suggesting an existing research engine.

Design source entries as a short reading list, not a spreadsheet, dense academic bibliography wall, or hover-only preview. Long titles and URLs must wrap without horizontal scrolling or forcing the font smaller.

## 12. Collaboration, privacy, and accountability

### 12.1 Invitation without a sales funnel

P7 can use a pale secondary surface and a stronger heading to signal a change from explanation to participation. It remains an informational section, not a full-bleed conversion billboard.

Contribution examples remain ordinary text with small, consistent bullets if helpful. Avoid role-selector cards, "join the movement" urgency, large participation counters, free-trial language, or scarcity cues.

The introductory action **Explore ways to contribute** and P6 action lead to P7. They are not direct email shortcuts. One prominent action per local group is enough; secondary reading/privacy actions remain clearly visible.

### 12.2 Warning → privacy → email

Within P7, use this single visual and reading order:

1. Contribution examples and realistic expectations.
2. "Before you email" heading and health-disclosure/non-clinical warning.
3. Privacy explanation and a prominent text link to U1.
4. Readable dedicated email address and the email action.
5. Optional-message guidance and approved recipient/response expectations.

The warning should be a calm, clearly bounded inset, with body-sized text and a modest boundary rule. It should not resemble a clinical alert, security guarantee, or emergency-service banner. No padlock icon that implies email confidentiality.

Privacy is not "fine print" under a dominant button. The email address must be manually selectable/copyable, with line wrapping that does not alter it. No copy-button dependency, email-obscuring puzzle, mail-client auto-launch, or sent-success notification.

Before the address exists, a mockup shows a clearly marked nonfunctional placeholder. Do not invent an address or conceal the unresolved status through polished styling.

### 12.3 Utility privacy page and footer

U1 uses the same body typography and content measure as the landing page, with a simpler heading and normal section spacing. No decorative hero is necessary. Operator, processing, retention, and request information should be scannable, not compressed into a long legal block.

The footer is concise, but contact/corrections, review date, and privacy links remain at readable size and contrast. Do not hide accountability behind a disclosure widget or make the footer look like an institutional seal strip.

No approved public operator, real email, final retention policy, or material relationship is invented for visual completeness. Those are publication facts to resolve separately.

## 13. Desktop and mobile composition

### 13.1 Shared composition

Use one content hierarchy and reading sequence across viewports. A desktop margin is an enhancement, not a place for essential text that disappears on mobile. Do not visually reorder content away from its intended reading order.

| Area | Desktop direction | Mobile direction |
|---|---|---|
| P0 masthead | Text identity and four navigation links in a calm horizontal arrangement where space permits. | Name above or beside naturally wrapping links; no required hamburger or sticky header. |
| P1 introduction | Left-aligned reading block; optional small abstract illustration in a subordinate outer area. | Text first; decorative illustration omitted or moved after the complete introductory package, never between definition and boundary. |
| Section map | Compact subordinate text navigation after the introduction. | Readable vertical list or wrapping groups with generous targets; do not shrink it to fit one row. |
| P2-P3 explanation | Stable text spine and vertically ordered concept modules. | Same order; no horizontal cards, carousels, or swipe instructions. |
| P4 initiative | Modest tonal emphasis; first-initiative heading and question remain text. | Comfortable inset or normal text layout; no narrow banner with oversized type. |
| P5 origin | Plain prose with optional quiet margin detail. | Concise third-person narrative without a visual timeline or image interruption. |
| P6 current work | One factual prototype note followed by subordinate future ideas. | Stack name, label, scope, limitation, date; do not squeeze metadata into a single row. |
| P7 collaboration | A generous reading area with the full warning/privacy/email sequence. | One continuous column; email does not jump above the warning or into a floating bottom bar. |
| P8-P9 | Readable references and concise accountability, not a tiny multi-column footer. | Wrap naturally; source and privacy links retain comfortable targets. |
| U1 | Quiet utility document in the same reading system. | Same content and access, not a reduced mobile policy. |

### 13.2 Composition stresses to examine later

Use the real provisional headline length, a long source title, an address placeholder with realistic length, multi-line status labels, and expanded user text settings in the mockup review.

Review at a representative desktop width, a narrow mobile width, and 320 CSS-equivalent pixels. Enlarged text and intermediate widths matter more than two attractive static screenshots.

The page should not rely on full-screen sections, precisely aligned line endings, pinned visuals, or excessive empty margins to work.

## 14. Accessibility and motion policy

### 14.1 Accessibility implications

The approved WCAG 2.2 AA target remains in force. This document and future mockups do not establish conformance.

Design review must consider:

- At least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and applicable 3:1 requirements for qualifying large text and functional non-text elements.
- Visible, unobscured keyboard focus on links, section navigation, source returns, and email actions.
- AA target-size requirements, with a preference for approximately 44 by 44 logical/CSS-equivalent pixels for primary actions where feasible.
- 200% text resizing, 320 CSS-pixel reflow, text-spacing adjustments, and no clipped warnings or statuses.
- Non-color cues for links, state, classification, and status.
- Meaningful heading levels and descriptive labels, not typography alone as hierarchy.
- No dependence on icon recognition, hover, precise dragging, timed interaction, or animation.
- Usable forced-colors/high-contrast treatment; do not suppress user preferences to preserve the palette.
- Reading order that keeps the privacy warning before email even when decoration is absent.

Typography, spacing, and focus decisions should be examined with intended users and relevant access needs. A diagnosis must not be used as a proxy for a person's needs or as justification for stereotyping the design.

### 14.2 Motion and animation

**Recommended baseline: static.** Express motion through composition and trajectories, not animation.

No animated signals, streaming dots, pulsing status, parallax, auto-playing video, scroll-linked diagrams, text reveal, loading theatrics, or smooth-scroll requirement. Content and contact instructions must appear immediately, without waiting for an effect.

Brief hover/press transitions may be explored later only as nonessential polish, approximately 100-150 milliseconds, with no delayed focus indication. They must respect reduced-motion preferences and remain unnecessary to understanding or task completion.

The first high-fidelity mockup should be judged as a static design. No animation is needed to make it feel modern.

## 15. Three distinct visual concepts

These concepts share the approved content, sequence, boundaries, and accessibility goals. They differ in composition, typographic character, surface treatment, and illustrative language, not in product scope. Names are internal design labels, not proposed public brands.

### Concept A: Fieldnotes Laboratory — recommended

**Character:** An open research notebook with contemporary editorial clarity.

**Composition:** A stable reading spine, generous but moderate section spacing, and occasional margin annotations. Fine rules and one abstract trajectory motif give structure without becoming a graphic system of fake measurements.

**Typography:** Sturdy contemporary serif headings with a humanist sans body. Technical notation is rare and subordinate.

**Color:** Warm paper, charcoal, deep teal, and a small earth accent. Light appearance; surfaces remain mostly neutral.

**Illustration:** Sparse static paths or contour fragments in the introduction margin. No axes, numerical labels, sensor shapes, or destination called a discovery.

**Section character:** P3 reads like three connected notes; P4 highlights the first question; P5 is a short account; P6 is a factual work note; P7 is a welcoming, bounded invitation.

**Strengths:**

- Balances scientific care, emerging-technology curiosity, and human readability.
- Works honestly with text and documentation rather than requiring impressive assets or product screenshots.
- Gives source context and limitations a natural home.
- Remains viable if decorative art is removed and if the headline changes.

**Weaknesses:**

- Can drift into a literary publication or lifestyle journal if the serif, paper warmth, or margins dominate.
- Incubator/technical depth may be under-signaled without precise status and source treatment.
- Sparse design can feel generic if alignment, typography, and small technical details are not carefully resolved.

**Guardrails:** No aged-paper texture, handwriting fonts, faux annotations, torn edges, decorative stamps, or simulated laboratory notebook entries. The design is contemporary, not nostalgic.

### Concept B: Signal Bench

**Character:** A clear technical workbench stripped of dashboard theater.

**Composition:** More compact, regular section bands with a visible structural grid expressed through alignment and a few clear rules. The reading spine remains singular; section indices can be slightly more prominent than in Concept A.

**Typography:** Readable sans serif for both headings and body, with strong but restrained weight contrast. A limited technical face can mark a section index; it does not become body text.

**Color:** Near-white mineral surface, graphite text, deep petrol interactive accent, and a sparse muted ochre detail. Not a dark terminal or neon interface.

**Illustration:** Small static schematic line studies or spaced markers, visibly abstract and not resembling physiological signals. No readings, chart axes, responsive sensor displays, or running-system indicator.

**Section character:** P3 uses disciplined stacked modules; P6 resembles a sober artifact record; P7 remains ordinary, human-language correspondence rather than a "connect endpoint."

**Strengths:**

- Makes the founder's practical engineering and experimentation ethos more visible.
- Supports precise labels, dates, and references without ornamental assets.
- Could appeal to technical contributors while retaining a readable structure.

**Weaknesses:**

- Highest risk of looking like enterprise software, a device portal, or an operational analytics product.
- Can make patients and families feel the site is primarily for engineers.
- Dense rules and metadata can distract from the common purpose and inflate apparent maturity.

**Guardrails:** No literal visible grid behind text, terminal vocabulary, code snippets, telemetry widgets, dials, status LEDs, metric tiles, or application-window framing. If technical styling reduces warmth or comprehension, simplify rather than add a friendly stock image.

### Concept C: Open Atlas

**Character:** A human exploration essay organized by questions, with restrained cartographic references.

**Composition:** Broad quiet surfaces and strong editorial landmarks; section changes feel like stages in an inquiry without becoming a route map. Typography provides most of the hierarchy. Preserve P1-P9 rather than inventing an expedition narrative.

**Typography:** Open humanist sans headings and body, with an optional readable serif for a small number of reflective question callouts. Less technical notation than A or B.

**Color:** Warm ivory, dark forest/ink, pale stone, and a restrained rust accent. Map-like tones, not outdoor-brand camouflage or a travel-poster palette.

**Illustration:** One abstract contour or branching path fragment with no place names, coordinates, user locations, numbered expedition milestones, or true geographic meaning.

**Section character:** P4's discovery question becomes an editorial landmark; P5's origin remains concise; P6 grounds the exploratory tone in actual documentation; P7 invites dialogue rather than a journey sign-up.

**Strengths:**

- Most direct visual expression of curiosity, trajectories, and human exploration.
- Can be inviting without medical imagery or artificial technical authority.
- Makes the long page feel intentionally paced rather than assembled from product cards.

**Weaknesses:**

- Can resemble an outdoor, cycling, travel, or personal-adventure site.
- Large landmarks and contour art may delay understanding or make the narrative too long.
- Scientific seriousness and incubator character are less immediate than in A or B.

**Guardrails:** No full-bleed cycling hero, adventure photography requirement, compass emblem, expedition badge, true ride map, or implied destination such as recovery. Do not promote the origin above the purpose.

### Comparison and recommendation

| Consideration | A: Fieldnotes Laboratory | B: Signal Bench | C: Open Atlas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leading impression | Human scientific inquiry | Practical technical investigation | Reflective exploration |
| Main design instrument | Reading typography and disciplined annotations | Alignment, restrained rules, precise labels | Editorial pacing and abstract cartographic space |
| Fit to documentation-only maturity | Strong; text is the natural artifact | Good, but software appearance needs restraint | Good, but origin can overshadow work |
| Main credibility risk | Looking more established/editorial than the project is | Looking like an operating measurement product | Looking like personal adventure rather than research inquiry |
| First-concept asset dependency | Low; optional abstract art only | Low; schematic decoration optional | Low if photography and large map art are excluded |
| Accessibility pressure point | Serif selection and overly subtle rules | Density, small labels, repeated boundaries | Oversized whitespace and decorative distraction |

These are qualitative design judgments, not audience findings or numeric scores.

**Recommend A because it best fits what exists now:** a carefully framed question, approved strategy, and a written content prototype. It can communicate rigor without pretending a platform is running and warmth without making the founder story the product.

Borrow only the clarity of B's work metadata and C's restrained sense of trajectory. Do not merge all three into a crowded hybrid. A remains the organizing direction, with the other concepts serving as meaningful alternatives rather than a menu of unrelated decorative features.

## 16. Examples of what to avoid

| Avoid | Why it fails here | Prefer |
|---|---|---|
| Doctor/clipboard hero, medical cross, hospital-blue navigation | Implies clinical authority and institutional service. | Purpose-first copy and neutral editorial structure. |
| Glowing brain, particle head, AI orb, gradient mesh hero | Makes technology the sales proposition and invites unsupported intelligence claims. | Small static abstract path, or no illustration. |
| Neon black interface with scan lines and futuristic panels | Resembles speculative fiction or a monitoring product. | Light reading surfaces and restrained technical annotation. |
| Fake symptom chart, gait trace, biomarker curve, exercise-response graph | Invents evidence and suggests validated interpretation. | Honest question text and clearly non-data decoration. |
| A graph merging public research and private health streams | Suggests a data integration the project has explicitly not authorized. | Written separation and explicit future governance boundaries. |
| Three color-coded "proof" badges | Equates categories with evidence strength or approval. | Full concept names and plain-language distinctions. |
| App screenshots in device frames for proposed tools | Makes ideas appear available or implemented. | A factual documentation/prototype note. |
| Giant "Join now" button above the warning | Creates a sales funnel and bypasses informed contact. | A section link followed by warning, privacy, and email context. |
| Tiny gray privacy text and a padlock next to email | Makes privacy easy to miss while implying unjustified protection. | Readable warning and accurate email-handling explanation. |
| Heroic founder portrait and achievement timeline | Turns the initiative into a biography and suggests representativeness. | Concise third-person origin within the approved order. |
| Dense dashboard tiles, metrics, progress bars, live dots | Implies operations and measurement unsupported by current work. | Status text, scope, limitation, and a real review date. |
| Franchise-like insignia, starship UI, or recognizable science-fiction visual language | Substitutes borrowed identity for the project's own character. | Generic exploration themes without derived imagery. |
| Long page of identical floating cards and shadows | Produces generic startup styling and fragments a connected explanation. | Stable prose spine, selective rules, and occasional quiet surfaces. |

## 17. Founder decisions before a high-fidelity mockup or implementation plan

The approved IA, email mechanism, documentation-only status, omitted variability sentence, and provisional-headline rule remain fixed. The following decisions concern visual direction and next-phase authorization, not reapproval of those constraints.

### 17.1 Before high-fidelity mockup work begins

| Decision needed | Recommended decision | What remains open afterward |
|---|---|---|
| Select the organizing concept | Approve A: Fieldnotes Laboratory; do not combine all three concepts equally. | Exact compositions and audience feedback may refine the direction. |
| Approve type character | Explore sturdy serif headings plus humanist sans body, with no custom wordmark. | Exact families, licenses, fallbacks, sizes, and legibility checks. |
| Approve palette character | Warm neutral light surfaces, charcoal, deep teal, and a restrained earth accent. | Final values and all contrast/state checks; no dark-mode variant in the initial scope. |
| Approve density and layout character | Stable reading spine, moderate whitespace, no oversized full-screen hero or horizontal concept cards. | Exact measures, responsive behavior, and intermediate-width refinement. |
| Approve illustration boundary | One optional, static, abstract non-data trajectory treatment; no photography requirement. | Exact art and permissions if any later real asset is proposed. |
| Approve semantic treatments | Equal reading comfort for the three concepts; neutral status labels; source links as readable text, not credibility decoration. | Final component appearance and real factual content verification. |
| Approve participation emphasis | Gentle surface distinction at P7, warning/privacy visually intact before email, no privacy fine print or clinical-alert styling. | Actual address, operator, retention, and response expectation. |
| Approve static-first policy | No decorative animation, parallax, streaming signals, or content reveal. | Any later nonessential state transition must meet the accessibility constraints. |
| Authorize the mockup scope | Nonfunctional desktop and mobile landing-page compositions plus the U1 privacy utility layout, using approved prototype copy and conspicuous review placeholders. | Narrow-width, enlarged-text, keyboard/focus/state specifications and later implementation evaluation. |

Final headline selection is **not** required to begin the mockup: use the approved working candidate and demonstrate its flexibility. Do not fill unresolved content with invented scientific findings, partner marks, software artifacts, or a real-looking contact identity.

### 17.2 Before implementation planning begins

The founder must separately accept the refined visual mockup and its relationship to the approved requirements; authorize the implementation-planning scope, owner, available capacity, and budget; and decide how audience/accessibility findings will affect the next phase.

Confirm the design's content and interaction states, accessibility acceptance approach, performance/asset limits, and required operating inputs. Track unfinished source verification, public-story permissions, contact ownership, privacy disclosures, and final-copy approval as explicit gates rather than silently closing them through design approval.

Any technology selection needs separate authorization in that later phase and must follow the requirements. Approval of this visual-direction document or a future mockup does not itself select a stack, authorize production code, approve a logo, or permit collecting personal health data.
