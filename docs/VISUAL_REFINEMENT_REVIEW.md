## 3. Three shorter headline alternatives

The previous working headline is 71 characters, including spaces and punctuation:

> Exploring change through evidence, experience, and emerging technology.

| Alternative | Characters | Strength and tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Explore change: evidence, lived insight, new tools.** | 51 | Recommended and rendered. Keeps exploration/change and all three subject areas in a concise editorial statement. The colon and abbreviated list are less conversational than the original. |
| **B. Explore change with evidence, lived insight, and new tools.** | 59 | A complete, more conversational sentence with the same inquiry. Shorter than the original but less concise than A. |
| **C. Change over time: evidence, lived insight, new tools.** | 53 | Makes the longitudinal purpose explicit. More like a topic statement than an invitation to explore. |

These are shorter **working alternatives**, not final headlines. A is approximately 28% shorter than the previous candidate and is the only alternative composed into page frames in this pass.

### Meaning and precision

The headline's abbreviated language must be read with the unchanged definition immediately below it:

> The Emergent Collaborative explores how scientific evidence, lived experience over time, and emerging technology might help people ask better questions about health.

The inquiry remains exploration of change, not a claim that tools or personal observations demonstrate a medical effect. In the shorter headlines:

- **Evidence** remains explicit.
- **Lived insight** is headline shorthand for the lived-experience perspective, not a replacement name for Experience or a new grade of scientific support.
- **New tools** is shorthand for emerging technology under investigation, not a statement that software or services are available.

There is a real precision tradeoff: neither abbreviated phrase is a complete synonym for its longer counterpart in every context. Meaning is preserved by the introductory package, not by pretending the headline alone says everything. The existing definition is therefore essential, not optional supporting text. Evidence / Experience / Exploration and their limitations remain unchanged in P3.

If the founder requires the literal words "experience" and "technology" inside the large headline, that is a different copy constraint: those words cannot simply be kept at the current size and assumed to fit every narrow measure. This proposal resolves the specific word-length problem editorially rather than hiding it, condensing the font, or cancelling user enlargement.

PH-15 remains visible and unchanged in every revised P1 view. The content prototype's previous headline is not silently replaced or treated as final copy.

The focus outline around Parkinson's initiative in the comparison specimens illustrates the existing 3-pixel earth ring and 3-pixel clear offset. It is not an active-page indicator or a claim that keyboard navigation works in an image. The updated complete landing frames use the ordinary, unfocused appearance.

## 8. Verdict and remaining approval blocker

**Headline concern: resolved in the revised static compositions at 390 and 320, including 200% text, without smaller type or within-word headline breaks.**

**Navigation concern: addressed at ordinary 768, 390, and 320 through lower-overhead wrapping. At 200%, returning to four rows is deliberate readable behavior, not an unresolved failure to make the navigation compact.** The full page is still long; this bounded pass does not claim to solve all reading effort by compressing unaffected content.

**Remaining blocker to final visual approval: the founder must accept the proposed headline wording and the content-fitting wrapping treatment, then explicitly approve the refined visual package.** In particular, accept or reject the precision tradeoff of "lived insight" / "new tools" alongside the unchanged exact definition. Choosing a different candidate, or requiring the original long terms in the large headline, would need its own visual review rather than an assumed pass.

No additional headline/navigation geometry defect was observed in the rendered anchors. Later audience/accessibility evaluation is still required; no static fit result closes that work. Existing operational, privacy, publication, font-delivery, contrast, and rights placeholders remain unresolved gates where applicable, not facts this refinement can fabricate or a demand to resolve all operating choices before visual approval.

Final visual approval has not been presumed. No production code, implementation planning, frontend-framework selection, or hosting-provider selection has begun.
